# Part II experimental plan — LLM-designed control of one vehicle

**Research question.** Can a rule designed by an LLM using simulator feedback reduce speed fluctuations behind one controlled vehicle while preserving or improving whole-cohort travel time on previously unseen arrival sequences?

## Feasibility check

An all-IDM pilot used coursework experiment 4, its 0.1 s update, a 60-vehicle demand cap, a persistent bottleneck and a 1,200 s observation horizon. The original generation procedure supplied the scheduled arrivals. The cap permits complete trip measurement. Vehicles 31–40 define the follower cohort behind designated vehicle 30. The two pilot seeds are public development data. No policy was trained. Times below exclude Python startup and plotting.

## Protocol to freeze before candidate search

1. **Build a paired benchmark.** Generate demand schedules, bottleneck eligibility flags and relative-speed noise independently of the policy. Index noise by vehicle and simulation step. Share the same inputs across all arms for each seed. The original simulator uses a shared random stream, so an identical seed alone does not ensure pairing. Replay an identical no-intervention policy through both execution paths and require identical vehicle trajectories. Check these tests before ranking policies. The paired baseline may differ from the pilot because the random streams change.
2. **Define what a candidate controls.** Vehicle 30 can keep normal following, apply an upstream target-speed cap or release it. All other vehicles use IDM. Reassess the high-level action every simulated second. Keep the 0.1 s IDM dynamics, following constraints and the underlying road target; apply the lower of the road target and any policy cap. Give all policies identical current ego/leader observations, recent detector speed/flow summaries and their own intervention state. Fix detector locations and feature definitions on development data. Encode missing observations explicitly. No future observations, seed or aggregate scores enter the running rule. Restrict generated rules to validated expressions or a small decision table and log requested and applied actions.
3. **Set metrics and reward.** Use the same 60 scheduled vehicles and wait for all exits; flag non-completion at the cap and never score only completed trips. T is the sum of exit time minus scheduled arrival over the full cohort. W is the sum of absolute stepwise speed changes for vehicles 31–40 divided by their total distance in km. C sums on-road vehicle-seconds below 5 m/s. M is the elapsed time from the common start until the last exit. Keep the exit count at 400 s and minimum raw net gap as diagnostics. Compute net gaps from synchronized positions before the leader exits. Record collisions and minimum-gap violations separately.
4. **Score and reject.** For metric X compute I_X = 100 × (X_IDM − X_policy) / X_IDM on the same seed. Provisional reward R = 0.5 I_T + 0.3 I_W + 0.1 I_C + 0.1 I_M. If a denominator is zero, report absolute changes and mark that score undefined; resolve such a case before model selection. Provisional eligibility: all vehicles finish, no overlap, minimum sampled active net gap at least 2 m on every evaluation seed. Verify that the benchmark IDM itself meets the declared threshold in development; report any baseline failure. Keep raw metrics for each seed. Define joint improvement in advance as lower mean paired T and lower mean paired W; a positive composite reward alone does not establish both.
5. **Run controls and LLM search.** Include all-IDM, an explicit hand-written He-inspired slow-in/release rule, and the frozen LLM rule. Treat this as a course adaptation of JAD. All arms use the same designated vehicle. Let the LLM propose three symbolic rules per round, with three rounds on development seeds 1, 2, 3. After each round return metric breakdowns plus a trace excerpt of a poor decision. Preserve the original prompt, model/version, response, executable rule, score and rejected candidates. A recorded dialogue with the LLM suffices for a manual offline prototype. If comparing the benefit of rule structure, give the hand-written rule nine predeclared parameter settings with the same simulation budget as the nine LLM candidates.
6. **Select and freeze.** Retain at most two finalists per search method by mean feasible development reward. Use validation seeds 11 and 12 to choose one per method. Freeze rules, weights, safety checks, cohort, detectors and selection criteria. Use unopened test seeds 101–106 once to report paired differences and the number of seeds improved. These tests concern arrival and noise variation within experiment 4. Six seeds support an exploratory comparison.

## Deliverables and stopping conditions

First milestone: matched all-IDM replay plus one hand-written intervention with a visible trigger and release. Second milestone: a complete LLM proposal, evaluation, failure diagnosis and revised proposal on matched inputs. Third milestone: frozen test comparison, per-seed metrics, rule history, and time–space diagrams with identical axes and speed scales. Retain a failing example if one occurs. Development reward need not improve; a reproducible negative result answers the research question.
